## SLIDE 6 — What the Data Reveals

**Headline:** Six findings that change how we'd price this product

- Coupon users renew **9 points lower** than full-price learners
- Power Learners renew at **86.7%** vs. **19.6%** for Casual Learners
- Working Professionals generate the **highest revenue per learner**
- Pro plan outperforms Standard on revenue, despite fewer subscribers
- Support tickets fall from **2.8 to 0.5** as completion rises
- College Students over-index on discounts — **57%** redemption rate

**Visual:** 6 insight cards in a 3x2 grid, bold stat top, one-line takeaway below.

## SLIDE 7 — How the Engine Decides ⭐ (Anchor Slide)

**Headline:** 22 rules. 14 parameters. Zero machine learning.

**Visual (primary — vertical flow, 6 stages):**
Learner Data → SQL Analysis → 22 Business Rules → 14 Pricing Parameters → Recommendation Engine → **Plan · Discount · Renewal Offer**

**Why SQL, not ML (3 lines max):**
- Fully explainable — every decision traces to a named rule
- No training data required for a new product
- Priority-ordered — churn risk is checked before any upsell

**Visual note:** Make this the most visually dominant slide in the deck — large flow diagram, generous whitespace, small "0 ML models used" badge.

## SLIDE 12 — What's Next

**Headline:** Built for today, designed to extend

**Roadmap (3 horizontal stages):**
- **Now:** SQL rules engine — transparent, explainable, zero training data required
- **Next:** A/B test rule-driven recommendations against a control group
- **Later:** Layer in ML for edge cases the rules can't resolve — real-time recommendations, dynamic pricing, predictive churn scoring

**One line on the SQL choice:** *SQL was chosen deliberately — for a new product with no historical outcome data, every rule can be justified individually. ML earns its place once that data exists.*

- **Charts:** Single-color blue scale, direct data labels on bars instead of legends where possible
